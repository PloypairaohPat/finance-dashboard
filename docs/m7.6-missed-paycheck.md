# M7.6 PR 6 — the missed-paycheck alert: audit and plan

The rule as agreed: only a stored **inflow stream with a salary code** (`INCOME_SALARY`;
`INCOME_WAGES` where a v1 Item still sends it — never the `INCOME` primary, never interest or
dividends), **`MATURE` and active**, on a **fixed frequency** (weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly,
monthly), and only for users who **opt in**. It must never fire on irregular income.

This is an audit: nothing in `src/` or the schema changed. It adds a read-only backtest,
`backend/scripts/missed-paycheck-backtest.ts`, with its pure parts in `scripts/lib/`
(`businessDays.ts`, `paycheckBacktest.ts`) and a test for them.

## 1. What the old `missedPaycheck` did, and why it was removed

From git history (`72884452`, M5.8; removed in `44a2ae9d`) and `docs/m7.3-consumer-audit.md`:

- **It never fired.** It read `subscriptionAnalysis.incomeStreams` through an `any` cast; no
  such field ever existed, so its input was always empty. Behind that, the mapping it would have
  read dropped `predicted_next_date`.
- **Its input was a Plaid call on every bell open**, swallowed to `null` on failure, so a Plaid
  outage would have made it answer "nothing missing" and resolve its alerts by absence.
- **It took every inflow stream:** interest, dividends and transfers in, as well as pay. No
  salary code, no maturity, no frequency check, no opt-in.
- **Its grace was 3 calendar days**, so a payday before a weekend could fire on Monday.
- **"Arrived" meant any income row** whose label contained the stream's merchant name — and with
  no merchant name, which is normal for payroll, *any* income in the window counted: interest, a
  tax refund, or an unpaired transfer in from savings could hide a missing paycheck. Before
  M7.3, any negative amount counted at all.
- **Its fingerprint was `missed_paycheck:${ym}`:** one alert per month for everything, so two
  missed biweekly paydays in a month were one alert, and it named no stream.

What the new one keeps from M7.3's notes: on real data every salary deposit carried a different
label, so **payer identity can't come from the label**; contractor income isn't scheduled and isn't
a paycheck; and it's a **state alert** that resolves by absence.

## 2. How lateness is judged

**Expected date.** Plaid's `predicted_next_date`, which we store as
`RecurringStream.predictedNextDate`. When Plaid sends none, or its date is no later than the
stream's last deposit (a prediction from before that deposit, not yet refreshed), our own: the next
payday on the stream's nominal schedule — its usual day of the month (one in each half for
semi-monthly; any of the month's last three days read as "month end"), or its usual weekday phase.
The backtest prints how often Plaid's date and ours agree; it can't judge history against Plaid's
date, because Plaid only ever gives the next one.

**Grace, in banking days.** Counted from the banking day on or after the expected date, skipping
weekends and Federal Reserve holidays (`scripts/lib/businessDays.ts`: a Sunday holiday is
observed Monday; a Saturday one isn't moved, so the Friday before stays a banking day). Pay sent
early for a weekend or holiday is on time. **Grace: 2 banking days** (decided after the
backtest). One would risk firing on ordinary ACH posting delays; three waits most of a week
after a Friday payday.

**What counts as arrived.** Income deposits carry no entity ids, and their labels vary, so a
deposit is matched to its stream by where and how much, not by name. A deposit counts for an
expected date if it is:

- **in the stream's account** (`RecurringStream.plaidAccountId`),
- **classifier income** (never a transfer between the user's own accounts, a refund or a
  payment-app repayment), **and** either in the stream's `plaidTransactionIds` or coded with a
  salary code,
- **dated from half a pay period before the expected date to the deadline**, and
- **at least half the stream's usual amount** (the median of its recent deposits), with **no
  upper limit**. A paycheck with a bonus or overtime must never read as missing — a false
  "hasn't arrived" is the worst failure here — while a small reimbursement from the same
  employer still doesn't stand in for pay.

The stream's own transaction ids alone aren't enough: a deposit joins them only at the next
stream refresh, while it reaches our transactions at the sync. The backtest counts how often a
salary deposit outside the stream would have satisfied a payday the stream's own deposits missed.

**A pending deposit counts.** The alert is about money not showing up; a pending payroll deposit
has shown up. When it posts it gets a new id, but by then the posted row matches the same way.

## 3. Stale data

The alert must never fire because we're behind. **Proposed rule:** judge an expected date only
when, for the stream's Item,

- `lastSyncedAt` is after the deadline (transactions are current past it), **and**
- `streamsRefreshedAt` is after the deadline (the stream, and its predicted date, are current
  past it), **and**
- the Item is `healthy` (not `login_required` or in error).

Otherwise the detector says nothing new about that stream: it doesn't fire, and an alert it
already raised stays as it is (the detector re-emits an existing `missed_paycheck` it can't
judge, rather than letting resolution-by-absence clear it). `plaidUpdatedAt` isn't used: when
Plaid omits `updated_datetime` we store the refresh time instead, so it can't tell stale from
fresh.

With the daily backstop and the Sync button refreshing streams, a healthy Item is current within
a day, so this delays an alert by at most a day.

## 4. The opt-in

Modelled on `paymentAppInflowsAreIncome`: a `User` column, read and written through
`PUT /user/settings`, with a toggle in the Settings dialog. **It needs a column**
(`missedPaycheckAlerts Boolean @default(false)`), so by our rule PR 6 is two PRs:

- **6a — the column**, default off. Nothing reads or writes it: the settings API and the bell
  behave exactly as before.
- **6b — the behaviour:** the settings API and toggle, the detector, the demo fixture.

Off means the detector emits nothing for that user, so any alert it raised resolves by absence
on the next run.

## 5. Severity, wording, and one alert per payday

- **Severity: medium.** It can matter (rent, a bounced payment), but a late deposit is often
  harmless, and the user can act only by checking with their employer.
- **Title:** "Your paycheck from {payer} hasn't arrived". `{payer}` is the stream's merchant
  name or description.
- **Body:** "It's usually paid around {Fri 2 Oct} and hasn't reached {account} 2 banking days
  later. Deposits are sometimes late, and a holiday or a change at work can move a payday. This
  clears when it arrives."
- **Fingerprint: `missed_paycheck:{plaidAccountId}:{expected date}`.** One alert per payday. Keyed
  on the account rather than Plaid's `stream_id`, because a stream Plaid regroups gets a new id
  and would raise the same payday twice; two employers paying the same account on the same day
  is the one case this collapses, and it's rare.
- **It resolves** when pay arrives, when the stream stops qualifying (inactive, no longer
  mature), when the user turns the setting off, or when the stream's next payday arrives (that
  one is judged on its own), so a job change can't leave a permanent alert.

## 6. The demo

Visitors can't change settings (demo mode hides the dialog and refuses writes), so the demo
**ships with the setting on** and a fixture that shows the alert:

- The demo's salary (the 1st and the 15th) becomes a semi-monthly inflow stream, on time.
- A **second, biweekly employer** whose last expected payday is 7 calendar days before the build
  date, with no deposit: at least 3 banking days past it whatever the day of the week, so the bell
  shows the alert after every reseed. It resolves at the stream's next payday, a week after the
  reseed, the way the price-rise fixture ages out.
- The demo's Items get `lastSyncedAt` and `streamsRefreshedAt` stamped at the build time, so the
  stale-data rule doesn't withhold it.
- Like 5b's fixtures, these add rows the demo's income and transaction list show at the reseed.

## The backtest

`scripts/missed-paycheck-backtest.ts`, read-only and counts-only:

```
railway run npx tsx scripts/missed-paycheck-backtest.ts --allow-remote <db host>
```

Per user it prints:

- inflow streams by qualification (and why not)
- qualifying streams by frequency, and their pending deposits
- every past payday their deposits imply, by how pay came: on time, late by 1, 2, 3 or 4+ banking
  days, never
- how many of those the rule would have fired on at grace 1, 2 and 3
- how many of the fired ones a salary deposit outside the stream would have satisfied
- Plaid's predicted next payday against ours
- what the rule would say today on Plaid's date: not due, arrived, would fire, or withheld because
  data is behind

**Limits:**

- History is judged only up to each Item's last sync, assuming the data was current then. We don't
  keep past sync times, so the stale-data rule can't be replayed.
- The nominal schedule is inferred from all of a stream's deposits, hindsight a live alert won't
  have.
- M7.6's audit found the salary rule selects one stream across both users, so this is a small
  sample: it can show the rule firing wrongly, not prove it right.

**Results (production, first run).** One stream qualified across both users: biweekly. Every
past payday its deposits imply arrived on time, so the rule would have fired on none of them at grace 1, 2 or 3,
and Plaid's predicted next payday matches ours.

What this shows: on our one real paycheck the rule raises no false alarm, at any of the three
graces, and our expected date agrees with Plaid's. What it can't show: a late or missing payday
has never happened in our data, so the alert's firing path is exercised only by tests (below).

## Plan

- **6a — column:** `User.missedPaycheckAlerts`, default false, existing users included. No
  behaviour: the settings API neither returns nor accepts it yet.
- **6b — behaviour:**
  - `lib/businessDays.ts` (moved from `scripts/lib`, with its test)
  - the `missedPaycheck` detector on stored inflow streams per sections 2 and 3, registered for
    `missed_paycheck`, reading only stored data
  - `PUT` and `GET /user/settings` with the new field, and the Settings toggle
  - the demo's two inflow streams, the setting on, and the Item stamps
  - the demo checklist
  - `what-changed-for-you.md`
  - **Tests 6b must have.** Real data has never had a late payday, so these are the only proof
    that the alert fires when it should and never when it shouldn't. Each is broken once on
    purpose.
    - **Timing:**
      - it fires only after the deadline: the payday plus 2 banking days
      - pay moved early for a weekend is on time
      - a Federal Reserve holiday shifts the deadline
    - **Amount:**
      - a paycheck well above usual (a bonus, overtime) counts as arrived
      - one under half the usual doesn't
      - a pending deposit counts as arrived
    - **Stale data:**
      - held back when the Item's last sync is before the deadline
      - held back when its last stream refresh is before the deadline
      - held back when the Item is unhealthy
      - an alert already raised stays while it's held back
    - **Never fires:**
      - for interest or transfers in
      - for irregular income
      - for an inactive stream
      - for a stream without a fixed frequency
      - with the setting off
    - **One alert:**
      - one per account and payday
      - it resolves when pay arrives
      - it resolves when the setting is turned off
      - it resolves at the next payday
    - **Isolation:**
      - reads only the user's own rows and streams
      - the bell calls no Plaid endpoint
    - **The demo:**
      - the overdue fixture fires at every build date across a year
      - its on-time salary stream never does
- **Decided:**
  1. Grace: 2 banking days.
  2. Amount: a floor at half the usual amount, no upper limit.
  3. The fingerprint is keyed on the account and the payday, not the stream id.
  4. Severity: medium.
  5. The demo: the setting on, with an overdue second employer.
