# M7.3 — the transaction classifier

What every money figure in the app is computed from: one ordered classifier, first
match wins. It replaces the three definitions of "spend" and two of "income" recorded
in `m7.3-data-trust-notes.md`.

This revision follows calibration round 2. The queries are `m7.3-calibration.sql` and
`m7.3-calibration-2.sql`; their **results are deliberately not in this repo** — they are
a behavioural profile of real people and this repo is public. They live in
`docs/calibration-results/`, which is gitignored. Statements below say what calibration
showed, never the per-user counts behind it.

Decisions **D1**–**D8**. All are signed off except **D1**, which is waiting on a look at
one real transaction (open questions, below). The seed (`prisma/demo-dataset.ts`) tags
every fixture whose expected outcome depends on a decision, so reversing one shows up as
a failing, named test rather than silent drift.

## Inputs

Per transaction: account type (`depository` / `credit`), direction (Plaid sign:
positive = money out), `categoryPrimary` / `categoryDetailed`, the
`personal_finance_category.confidence_level`, `counterparties[]` (`name`, `type`), and
the user's linked institution names. Institution names are compared normalised
(lowercased, non-alphanumerics stripped).

"Linked-bank counterparty" means: a counterparty of type `financial_institution` whose
normalised name equals a linked institution's. It proves the **institution**, never that
the other account is linked. That distinction is the whole of D4.

## Rules, in order

**R0 — preconditions.** Soft-deleted rows are excluded. Pending rows are included
everywhere. A pending row superseded by its posted row (`pending_transaction_id`) is
dropped, as today.

**R1 — card payment (pair).** A depository outflow and a credit-account inflow of the
same user, amounts **equal to the cent**, dates **≤ 7 days** apart in either order, where
**neither leg carries a merchant, marketplace or payment_app counterparty (D2)**. Both
legs are card payments: never spend, never income.

Tolerance is exact, per calibration 2 section D: no near-misses exist in real data, so
nothing argues for a wider window. Not confidence-gated — the pairing is structural, and
calibration 1 showed card-payment categories are unreliable on both legs.

**R2 — internal transfer (pair).** A depository outflow and an inflow on a *different*
depository account, equal to the cent, **≤ 4 days** apart (**D8**), where **both legs
carry a transfer signal (D1)**. A transfer signal is a `TRANSFER_IN_*` / `TRANSFER_OUT_*`
code or a linked-bank counterparty. Both legs are internal: excluded from spend and income.

The window is 4 days for one specific reason: ACH does not settle over a weekend, so a
Friday transfer lands Tuesday — a 4-calendar-day gap. It is not general slack, and it
stops at 4: 5 days buys nothing in the seed and nothing in the calibrated data.

Not gated: the pairing corroborates the category.

**R3 — unpaired credit-account inflow.** A merchant or marketplace counterparty makes it
a **refund**; anything else is **neither spend nor income** (credit inflows are never
income). A refund nets against its own category when confidence passes the gate,
otherwise it reduces total spend as an unallocated refund (**G**).

**D7** extends refunds to depository inflows with a merchant or marketplace counterparty
and a spending primary category (not `INCOME_*`, not `TRANSFER_IN_*`). Without D7 a debit
refund lands in unclassified inflows.

**R4 — payment apps.** Any remaining row with a `payment_app` counterparty. Outflows are
**spend in a bucket of their own, "Payments to people"**. Inflows **reduce that bucket,
capped at the same period's payment-app outflow total**; anything above the cap is an
**unclassified inflow, shown, never income (D5)**. The UI shows the raw pair, not just the
net: `Payments to people: $X out, $Y in`.

Calibration 2 section B is why this is a bucket rather than a round-trip: payment-app
inflows substantially exceed outflows, so netting without a cap would manufacture negative
spend. If a row carries both a merchant and a payment_app counterparty, the merchant wins
and it is an ordinary purchase — an app used as a payment rail, not a payment to a person.

**R5 — unpaired card-payment outflow.** A depository outflow coded
`LOAN_PAYMENTS_CREDIT_CARD_PAYMENT` that R1 did not pair: a **card payment** if it has a
linked-bank counterparty **at an institution where a credit account is actually linked
(D3)**, otherwise **spend (Debt)**.

D3 closes the unlinked-card trap: paying a card at your bank that you have *not* linked
would otherwise be excluded, which hides the only evidence of that card's spending.
Gated (**G**) — the category is the only thing claiming this is a card payment.

**R6 — remaining depository inflows.**
- An **allowlisted account-transfer code** (below) **with a linked-bank counterparty** is
  an unpaired internal-transfer leg: excluded (**D4**, gated).
- `INCOME_*` and any other `TRANSFER_IN_*` count as **income**, per definition (c). This is
  the branch the user explicitly exempted from the gate: interest paid by your own bank,
  and cash deposits, stay income even though the counterparty is a linked bank.
- Everything else — `OTHER_OTHER` and friends — is an **unclassified inflow, shown**.

**R7 — remaining outflows.** **Spend**, in `mapPlaidCategory`'s bucket, except:
- an **allowlisted account-transfer code with a linked-bank counterparty** → internal,
  excluded (**D4**, gated);
- `TRANSFER_OUT_SAVINGS` or `TRANSFER_OUT_INVESTMENT_AND_RETIREMENT_FUNDS` with no linked
  counterparty → **moved to savings**, excluded from spend (gated, **G**). Below the gate
  it is spend.

### The allowlist (D4)

Exclusion on a linked-bank counterparty applies **only** to account-to-account codes:

```
TRANSFER_OUT_ACCOUNT_TRANSFER   TRANSFER_IN_ACCOUNT_TRANSFER
TRANSFER_OUT_SAVINGS            TRANSFER_IN_SAVINGS
TRANSFER_OUT_INVESTMENT_AND_RETIREMENT_FUNDS
                                TRANSFER_IN_INVESTMENT_AND_RETIREMENT_FUNDS
```

`TRANSFER_OUT_OTHER_TRANSFER_OUT` and `TRANSFER_IN_OTHER_TRANSFER_IN` were on this list
and have been **removed**. Every member requires a linked-bank counterparty *as well as*
the code — the allowlist is only ever consulted by R6/R7, which is the unpaired-leg
branch — but these two are Plaid's catch-all for transfers, which is where a miscoded
payment or a bank-branded P2P lands, so a name match is too weak a reason to delete them
from the picture. They remain valid transfer signals for R2, where a real second leg has
to exist. Removing them changes **no row in the calibrated data**: user 2's unpaired
`TRANSFER_IN_OTHER_TRANSFER_IN` rows carry no financial-institution counterparty at all,
and user 1's single one is on a credit account and pairs.

Everything else with a linked-bank counterparty is real money moving and stays counted.
This is the fix for the trap calibration 2 found — cash withdrawals name your own bank as
the counterparty — and it generalises, because the same shape appears elsewhere in the
same data:

| Shape | Counterparty | Correct answer |
|---|---|---|
| `TRANSFER_OUT_WITHDRAWAL` (cash out) | your bank | spend |
| `TRANSFER_IN_DEPOSIT` (cash in) | your bank | income |
| `INCOME_INTEREST_EARNED` | your bank | income |
| `BANK_FEES_*` (ATM, overdraft, monthly) | your bank | spend |
| `LOAN_PAYMENTS_*` on a loan held at your bank | your bank | spend |
| `TRANSFER_OUT_TRANSFER_OUT_FROM_APPS` via a bank-branded P2P | your bank | spend |

Withdrawals, deposits and interest all occur in the calibrated data; the last three are
reachable shapes that do not. An allowlist fails safe for codes nobody has seen yet: a new
or unexpected code is counted rather than silently dropped.

## The confidence gate (G, D6)

The gate applies **only where a rule depends on Plaid's category being right**, and each
rule's threshold is set by **which way that rule fails**:

- A rule that **removes spend** when it fires flatters the numbers if it fires wrongly.
  Those need **HIGH+** (`VERY_HIGH`, `HIGH`).
- A rule whose misfire **leaves the money counted**, or only keeps it out of income, can
  take **MEDIUM+** (`VERY_HIGH`, `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`).

`LOW`, `UNKNOWN` and a missing level never pass either.

| Rule | Firing wrongly… | Direction | Threshold | Below it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R3 refund netting | removes spend from a named category | flatters that category | **HIGH+** | still a refund, unallocated: total spend drops, no category is credited |
| R5 linked-bank branch | excludes a payment from spend | flatters | **HIGH+** | spend (Debt) |
| R7 savings exclusion | excludes an outflow from spend | flatters | **HIGH+** | spend |
| **D4 outflow** (R7) | excludes an outflow from spend | flatters | **HIGH+** | spend |
| **D4 inflow** (R6) | removes an inflow from income | conservative — understates income, cannot flatter spend | **MEDIUM+** | unclassified inflow, shown |

D4 splits because its two directions fail differently — that is the answer to "which way
does it fail": outward, a wrong exclusion hides spending; inward, it only makes the user
look poorer. Note that on the inflow side both outcomes keep the money out of income, so
the threshold there only decides the label the user sees, not the totals.

**Not gated:** R1 and R2 (structural pairing), R4 (keyed on counterparty type), and
definition (c)'s unmatched-inflow branch in R6.

## The payment-app cap and period boundaries (D5)

The cap is per period, on totals, not per transaction, so ordering inside a period does
not matter: a reimbursement arriving before the payment it repays still nets.

**The boundary case is real and does not self-correct.** Pay $80 on the last day of a
period, get repaid on the second day of the next: the first period keeps $80 of spend
forever, and the repayment either nets against that period's own payment-app outflows or
becomes an unclassified inflow.

**This accumulates; it is not a phase shift.** A phase shift would move spend between
periods and still total correctly. The floor at zero — a period's spend is
`max(0, out − in)`, never negative — means a surplus is never applied to any period's
spend, so every period where inflows exceed outflows leaves a permanent residue, and the
residues add up over successive periods. Measured on the seed's five completed periods:
$1,382.50 out and $1,286.25 in, which nets to **$96.25** across the whole span, but the
app reports the sum of per-period figures, **$209.75**. The $113.50 difference is exactly
the sum of the surpluses, and that identity holds generally:

```
Σ per-period spend  −  max(0, Σout − Σin)  =  Σ surplus
```

So the overstatement is bounded, always visible (it equals what the surplus line already
shows), and always in the same direction — but it grows with the number of periods in
which inflows exceed outflows, which for a habitual net receiver is most of them.

Three options. All three are implemented as pure functions in
`src/lib/paymentApp.ts` and measured in `tests/payment-app-cap.test.ts`, so whichever
ships, the cost of the others stays on record.

- **(a) Each period stands alone.** `spend = max(0, out − in)`; the surplus is shown and
  then dropped. Overstates by the sum of surpluses, permanently.
- **(b) Carry back** into the period that paid. Correct month to month, but it restates a
  closed period after budgets and alerts have already fired on it. Rejected on that basis.
- **(c) Carry forward.** `spendᵢ = max(0, outᵢ − inᵢ − carryᵢ)`, with the unused surplus
  becoming `carryᵢ₊₁`. Never restates: `carryᵢ` is fixed the moment period *i−1* closes.

**(c) does what it claims.** Σ spend = Σout − Σin + carry_final, and on the seed's five
periods it gives **$96.25** with zero carry left over, against (a)'s $209.75 — the whole
residue gone. Per period it reports 43.25 / 0 / 46.50 / 0 / 6.50.

**But it has two failure modes, both measured:**

1. **A windowed view imports error from outside the window.** The identity is an all-time
   property, and the app never shows all time — it shows the last N periods. Over the
   seed's last three periods the truth is $129.75; (a) reports $166.50, over by that
   window's own surplus, while (c) reports **$53.00**, under by $76.75 of carry earned
   *before* the window opened. On the numbers the app actually displays, (c) is further
   from the truth than (a), and wrong in the direction that flatters.
2. **The carry ratchets for a habitual net receiver** — which is precisely what the
   calibrated data looks like: payment-app inflows substantially outnumber outflows. Eight
   periods of being paid back build a credit of $560, and a ninth period in which $900
   genuinely goes out reports **$340**. The credit never expires, so a payment can be
   cancelled out by reimbursements earned arbitrarily long ago.

   **(c′)** bounds this by expiring a surplus after *n* periods: with *n* = 1 the same
   series reports $830, and the carry never exceeds one period's surplus. It costs the
   identity (expired carry is reported separately) and still leaves a period's figure
   depending on a number that is not on screen.

**Recommending (a)**, with the accumulation documented rather than corrected. The visible
`$X out, $Y in` line reconciles with the spend figure under (a) and only under (a); under
(c) or (c′) the reader also needs a carry they cannot see. (a)'s error is bounded, one
directional, never flattering, and equal to the surplus already displayed — and it is
correct on the windowed figures the app shows, which the all-time identity is not.

## Open questions

1. **The one real rent-shaped pair.** User 2 has a same-day, exact-amount pair across two
   of their own depository accounts, coded `RENT_AND_UTILITIES → TRANSFER_IN`. Under D1 it
   is rent (spend) plus an income transfer; under an either-leg rule both legs vanish.
   Net saved is identical either way — only spend and income move together. It is one
   transaction in your own data: worth looking at to settle whether Plaid miscoded an
   internal transfer as rent, or the user genuinely paid rent and was repaid.
2. ~~R2's 3-day window vs ACH weekends.~~ **Settled: widened to 4 days (D8).** What it
   costs, measured rather than guessed:

   - **In the calibrated data, nothing.** Every exact-amount depository↔depository pair
     found there is **0 days** apart, for both users; there is not a single pair in the
     1–7 day range. So widening neither catches nor breaks anything that exists today. It
     is insurance against a settlement pattern the current data does not contain.
   - **In the seed, two pairs appear at 4 days.** One is the intended catch: the
     Friday→Tuesday savings transfer, whose inflow was previously counted as income. The
     other is a genuine false positive: a rent-coded outflow carrying a linked-bank
     counterparty pairs with an unrelated same-amount inflow and vanishes from spend.
     That is the price, and it is now a named fixture (`rent-coincidence-4-day-cost`) so
     it can never disappear quietly.
   - **5 days adds nothing.** Re-running the same measurement at 5 and 7 days produces no
     further pairs in the seed, so 4 is the whole of the change.

   The false positive needs exact amount, both legs signalling, and a 4-day gap at once.
   If D1 later becomes an either-leg rule, this cost goes up sharply — the two decisions
   interact.
